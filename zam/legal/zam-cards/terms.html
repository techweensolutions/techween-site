1. What the ZAM Cards App Is

ZAM Cards (the "App") is a mobile application for iOS and Android published by Techween. It lets you hold digital ZAM cards issued to you by your bank, see their status and payment history, and pay merchants that accept ZAM by picking up the merchant's payment request (by scanning a QR code today; by sound or NFC where your bank enables them later) or opening a payment link, and approving the payment on your device.

The App is a window onto cards issued by your bank. Three separate relationships apply when you use it:

RelationshipGoverned by
You and your issuing bank — the card, your account, balances, limits, top-ups, fees, statements, blocking, and disputesYour bank's card terms and conditions
You and Techween — the App software itselfThese Terms
You and your bank — registering, requesting cards, activating cards and phones, link codesYour bank's ZAM Cards Manager app and its terms

Techween does not hold your money, does not decide whether a payment is approved, and does not know your account details. Your bank does.

3. Getting Started: Linking a Card

  1. Your bank issues you a ZAM card (debit, credit, or prepaid) and gives you an 11-character link code, as text or as a QR code, in its ZAM Cards Manager app. Link codes are single-use and expire 10 minutes after they are generated.
  2. You type or scan the code in the App. The App registers your device with your bank, receives a card credential, and creates a signing key pair on your device. The private key stays on your device; only the public key is sent to your bank.
  3. The App shows the card as Pending Authentication until your bank has verified your identity through its own process (some banks use the PACI mobile identity for this; the App itself does not perform identity verification), then Pending Activation until your bank activates the card and your device, then Active. Only an Active card can pay.
  4. You can link several cards from one or more banks, choose a default card, and unlink a card from your device at any time.

4. Paying with the App

  • A merchant creates a payment request and shows you a QR code (in store) or a ZAM Pay button and payment page (online). Where your bank has enabled it, the App can also pick up the payment reference as an inaudible tone from the merchant's device or page, or by NFC; every channel carries only the payment reference, and the steps below are the same.
  • You scan the code or open the link. The App shows you the merchant's name and logo, the amount in KWD, and any description, and lets you choose which card to pay with.
  • You confirm. The App asks for your device biometric (Face ID, Touch ID, fingerprint) or device passcode before approving every payment. This cannot be turned off: the payment key in your phone's secure chip will not sign without it. You can separately lock the App itself, and we recommend that you do.
  • The App signs the payment details with the private key created when you linked the card and sends the signed request to your bank. Your bank verifies the signature, checks your funds or limit, and approves or declines. The merchant is told the result within seconds.
  • A payment request can be approved only once and only while it is valid: 15 minutes at a till and 60 minutes online by default.

You are responsible for reviewing the merchant name and amount before approving. An approved payment is final between you and the merchant; refunds and disputes follow Section 8.

5. Your Responsibilities

  • Keep your device locked with a biometric or passcode and keep its operating system up to date. The App will not run on a rooted, jailbroken or modified device, and your bank will not link a card to an Android phone whose system software has been unlocked.
  • Do not share your link code, your device, or your unlock method with anyone. Anyone who can unlock your device and the App can approve payments from your cards.
  • Tell your bank immediately if your device is lost or stolen, if you suspect someone else has used your card, or if you see a payment you did not make. Your bank can block the card and deactivate the device at once.
  • Unlink your cards from a device before you sell, give away, or recycle it.
  • Use the App only for lawful purposes and only for your own cards.

6. Notifications and Connectivity

The App notifies you when a merchant cancels (voids) a payment, when a refund reaches your card, and when your bank switches a card or this phone on or off. It does not notify you of a payment you have just approved yourself; your bank may do so through its own channels. You start a payment yourself by picking up the merchant's payment request or opening a payment link, and the result and a receipt (reference, amount, date, fee and parties) are shown in the App, with thirteen months of history and monthly statements per card. Card status changes and payment history appear when you open the card or refresh the list. The App needs an internet connection to link cards and to pay.

8. Problems, Refunds, and Disputes

  • A payment failed or the merchant did not receive it: check the card's payment history in the App; if you were charged and the merchant shows no approval, contact your bank quoting the payment reference and, where shown, the confirmation code.
  • You want a refund: ask the merchant. A merchant can reverse a payment the same day or refund all or part of it within 180 days; the refund is credited to the same ZAM card by your bank.
  • You did not authorise a payment, or goods were not received or not as described: raise a dispute with your bank, which will investigate under its complaints process and the ZAM operating rules. Time limits apply, so raise it promptly.
  • The App itself is not working: contact us using the details in Section 15.

The ZAM Complaints Management Process describes how banks and Techween handle complaints and the timelines you can expect.
